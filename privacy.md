# PRIVACY POLICY

**Last updated November 23, 2026**

This Privacy Notice for unglazed ("**we**," "**us**," or "**our**") describes how and why we might access, collect, store, use, and/or share ("**process**") your information when you use our services ("**Services**"), including when you:

  * Download and use our mobile application Lightning TOPIK (the "**App**") on iPhone or Android, including its widgets, or any other application of ours that links to this Privacy Notice

  * Visit our website at [lightning-topik.com](https://lightning-topik.com) (the "**Site**")

  * Engage with us in other related ways, including any support conversations

**Questions or concerns?** Reading this Privacy Notice will help you understand your privacy rights and choices. If you do not agree with our policies and practices, please do not use our Services. If you still have any questions or concerns, please contact us at [ung@sai-korean.com](mailto:ung@sai-korean.com).

## SUMMARY OF KEY POINTS

**What personal information do we process?** Almost none. Lightning TOPIK has no accounts and no sign-up. The App does not collect your name, email address, phone number, contacts, or location from your device's location services, and the iPhone App does not collect Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA). We record usage events, and purchases through RevenueCat, under app-generated install identifiers that are not linked to your identity — see Section 1. For friend codes, the App registers each install with our own server under a random, anonymous token, and the server keeps the install's friend code and any code it claimed — see Friend codes in Section 1. On Android, the App also shows ads from Google AdMob, and Google's ads software collects the information it needs to serve them: your device's Android advertising ID, your IP address (from which Google may estimate your general location) and other device and ad-interaction information — see Section 2.

**Where is my study progress?** On your phone, with a copy in your own Apple or Google account. Your words, reviews, plan, exam date, and settings are stored in the App's storage on your iPhone or Android phone, which the App's widget also reads. So that your progress can follow you to a new phone, the iPhone App also keeps a copy in your own iCloud, and the Android App includes it in your phone's backup to Google. These copies are kept by Apple or Google under your account; we cannot see them, and a reinstall or a new phone restores your progress from them. We have no copy of your progress: our only server for the App, which runs friend codes, receives no study data — see Section 1.

**Do we use tracking or ads?** The iPhone App contains no advertising and no cross-app or cross-site trackers. It does not collect IDFA and shows no App Tracking Transparency prompt. The Android App is free and shows ads from Google AdMob until you buy Remove ads; where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), it asks for your consent before the first ad — see Section 2. Both apps use Mixpanel for product analytics (we read aggregated reports). It is not used for advertising or for tracking you across apps or websites, and the apps never use your advertising ID (IDFA or Android advertising ID) for it — Section 3 sets out who receives what.

**What about the website?** The Site has no accounts and sets no cookies. Cloudflare delivers it, and we count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which stores nothing on your device and does not identify you — see Section 1.

**How do I pay?** On iPhone, the Lifetime unlock is an Apple in-app purchase: Apple handles payment, and the App never receives your payment details or Apple ID. On Android, Remove ads is a Google Play in-app purchase: Google handles payment, and the App never receives your payment details or Google account; Google Play gives us order records only. Both apps record purchases with RevenueCat, a service that stores purchase records under a random install ID the App generates, not under your name or account — see Section 1.

**How do I delete my data?** Delete the App: that removes your study progress from your phone. The copy in your iCloud stays until you delete it in your iPhone's iCloud storage settings, and the copy in your Google backup follows your Google backup settings — see Section 6. To have RevenueCat's records, or our friend-code records, for your install deleted, email us.

## 1. WHAT INFORMATION DO WE PROCESS?

**Information you provide.** Lightning TOPIK requires no account and no sign-up. The App does not ask for, and does not collect, your name, email address, phone number, contacts, or location from your device's location services. The iPhone App collects no advertising identifier. On Android, the ads software in the App collects your device's advertising ID and IP address, as described in Section 2.

**Information stored on your device.** Your study data — the TOPIK level you choose, the words you study and how you rate them, review schedules, saved words, your study plan and exam date, reminder times, and settings — is stored in the App's private storage on your device. The App's widget (on iPhone, the lock screen widget; on Android, the Home screen widget) reads the same storage to show today's word. This data is not sent to us.

**Your progress in your own iCloud or Google backup.** So that a reinstall or a new phone can pick up where you left off, without an account:

  * **iPhone.** If you are signed in to iCloud, the App saves a copy of your study data, with the App's random install ID (described under Purchase records below) and the time of the save, in its private storage in your iCloud. It saves when you open or leave the App and after a study session, and also keeps one older copy, refreshed about once a week, as a safety net. This storage belongs to your Apple account: the App reads it only on devices signed in to that account, and we cannot read it. When the App starts on a phone with no progress, it reads this copy and restores your progress. Without iCloud, or with iCloud switched off for the App, nothing is saved there. Your phone's own iCloud Backup may also include the App's data.
  * **Android.** When backup is turned on in your Android settings, your phone's backup to Google includes the App's study data, the App's random install ID, and two small records of when the App last asked for a rating and suggested the widget. Facts about one phone are left out: the notification permission and reminder schedule, your ad consent choices, the cached purchase status, and the files of the ads, analytics and purchase software. On Android 12 and later, the App's data goes into the backup to Google only when it is end-to-end encrypted with your screen lock; without a screen lock it moves only in a direct transfer to a new phone. On Android 9 to 11, a phone without a screen lock may back it up to Google without end-to-end encryption. Android usually backs up about once a day, so the latest study may not be in the backup yet. When you set up a new phone from this backup, or reinstall the App, Android restores the App's data.

Apart from these copies in your own account and the online voice described under Device features, the only information that leaves your device is:

  * **Usage events.** The App reports usage events to Mixpanel, a product-analytics service: which screens and buttons are used in onboarding; study sessions, with counts of cards and of each grade (Again, Hard, Good or Easy); when the purchase screen is viewed and the result of a purchase, including the product, price and currency; friend-code actions, including the six-character friend codes involved; and, on Android, when an ad is shown, clicked or paid. Event details are fixed labels, codes and counts, never text you type. They are sent with the App's random install ID (the same one the App uses for purchase records, described under Purchase records below), your device model, OS and app version, the App's language, and an approximate location (city, region and country) that Mixpanel derives from your IP address (the App does not read your location). They do not include your name, email address, text you type, your advertising ID (IDFA or Android advertising ID) or the friend-code server's install token. They are not used for advertising or to track you across other apps or websites, and the iPhone App shows no App Tracking Transparency prompt. On Android, where the law requires your consent (for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), the App sends usage events only after your choice in Google's consent message allows it (purposes 1 and 8 of the IAB Transparency & Consent Framework); before that it sends nothing, and if you decline, analytics stay off. You can change this at any time in Profile › Privacy choices.
  * **Purchase information.** If you buy the Lifetime unlock on iPhone, payment is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store; if you buy Remove ads on Android, payment is handled entirely by Google through Google Play. The App checks your purchase status with Apple or Google on your device. The App does not receive your name, Apple ID, Google account, or payment details; on Android, Google Play gives us order records only.
  * **Purchase records (RevenueCat).** Both apps use RevenueCat, a service that records purchases for us. Each time the App opens, whether or not you have bought anything, it connects to RevenueCat with a random install ID that it generates and stores on your device (in the iPhone Keychain, or on Android in Block Store and the App's storage), so the ID usually survives a reinstall on the same device; the copy of your progress in your iCloud or Google backup carries it to a new phone, so your purchases stay under one ID. With it, RevenueCat receives the App version, platform, your device's OS version and model, your preferred languages, the IP address of the connection, on iPhone Apple's vendor identifier (IDFV), which RevenueCat's software sends with each request, and the records of any in-app purchases you have made (product, price, currency, store country, dates and the store's transaction IDs). The App also attaches the install ID to each purchase it starts with Apple or Google, and Apple and Google send RevenueCat notices of purchases and refunds of the App's in-app products with that ID. RevenueCat receives no advertising identifier (no IDFA or Android advertising ID), and no name, email, Apple ID, Google account, payment details or study data. We use this information to see sales, refunds and conversion reports per store and country; it is not used for advertising or tracking.
  * **Friend codes.** Each install of the App has a friend code. When three new installs claim one code, the four of them can buy Lifetime (iPhone) or Remove ads (Android) at a lower price for 30 days, as the [Terms of Use](terms.md) describe. For this, the App connects to our own friend-code server, which runs on Google Cloud (Firebase), when it opens and when you claim a code:
    * **Registration.** The first time, the App creates a random install token, saves it in the App's storage on your phone, and registers it with the server, together with the platform (iPhone or Android), the App version, the date the App was first opened on the phone, whether the App was on the phone before friend codes existed, and a device check (below). The server stores a one-way hash of the token as the install's anonymous ID, never the token itself. With that ID it keeps your friend code, the platform, when the install registered, the end of its 7-day window for claiming a friend's code, the result of the device check, and whether the App was on the phone before friend codes existed. It does not keep the App version or the first-open date.
    * **Claims.** If you claim a friend's code, the server keeps which code you claimed and when. For each code, it keeps the owner's anonymous ID, how many friends have claimed it and when the group filled. Other learners see your code only if you share it, and the server tells them only how many friends have joined it and whether its group is full, never who joined. On Android, if you installed the App from a friend's link, the App reads the code from Google Play's install referrer and fills it in; you still tap to claim it.
    * **Device check.** So that one phone cannot claim again and again, the App proves it is a new install. On iPhone, it sends a token from Apple's DeviceCheck: our server asks Apple whether this device has registered the App before and, if not, asks Apple to set one bit for the device. Apple keeps this bit for our developer account, and it stays when the App is deleted and reinstalled. The server also keeps a one-way hash of that device token with the install's anonymous ID. On Android, the App sends a Google Play Integrity token: our server asks Google to check it and reads only whether the App came from Google Play and runs on a genuine, certified Android device. Apart from that hash, the server does not keep the device-check tokens, only the result (a new install, a reinstall, or not verified).
    * **Daily totals.** The App reports when you open the share sheet for your code, and, at most once a day, when the lower price is shown to you. The server adds these, and the views of the link page, registrations, claims, filled groups and reopened codes, to totals per day that are not linked to any install.
    * **IP address.** Like any server, ours receives the IP address of each connection. It uses it only to limit how many requests one address can make per minute, keeps it briefly in memory and never writes it to its database; the server's own logs record errors, not IP addresses or tokens. Google Cloud may keep its own technical records of requests under its own policies.

    None of this includes your name, email address, Apple ID, Google account, advertising identifier or study data, and we do not link it to the App's other identifiers (the analytics and RevenueCat install ID). We use it only to give you a code, count claims, decide who can buy at the lower price, stop simple abuse (the same phone claiming twice, or claiming its own code), and count how much friend codes are used. The server and its database are in Google Cloud's asia-southeast1 region (Singapore). Deleting the App deletes the token from your phone but not the records on the server; Section 6 explains how to have them deleted.
  * **Ads on Android.** The information Google's ads software collects to show ads, described in Section 2.
  * **Support email.** If you contact us, we receive whatever you choose to include in your message.

**Device features.** On iPhone, word pronunciation is spoken by Apple's on-device speech synthesis. On Android, it is spoken by the text-to-speech engine set on your device (for example, Google's Speech Services); if that engine uses an online voice, the word being spoken is sent to the engine's provider to be read aloud. The App does not use the microphone or record audio. Study reminders are local notifications scheduled on your device, only if you allow notifications; we run no push notification server.

**Our website.** The Site has no accounts, no forms and no advertising, and it sets no cookies. Cloudflare delivers it: as with any website, your browser sends your IP address, the page you ask for, the page that referred you and your browser's user agent, and Cloudflare uses them to deliver the page and to protect the Site from attacks; we see only aggregated traffic reports. Each page also loads Cloudflare Web Analytics, a small script that reports the page you viewed, the page that referred you, your browser, operating system and device type, an approximate country, and how fast the page loaded. It stores nothing on your device (no cookies or local storage) and does not fingerprint or identify you; we see only aggregated counts. The Site's store buttons link to the App Store and Google Play with a label for the page they are on, so Apple and Google can report to us, in aggregate, how many installs came from the Site. A friend's link (lightning-topik.com/c/ followed by a code) asks our friend-code server on Google Cloud whether the code's group is full: your browser sends that request, with your IP address, to Google Cloud, and the server adds the view to a daily total, as described under Friend codes. The page's Google Play button passes the code to Google Play as the install referrer.

## 2. THE ANDROID APP: ADS, CONSENT AND GOOGLE PLAY

This section applies only to the Android App. The iPhone App shows no ads.

**Ads.** The Android App is free and shows native ads from Google AdMob in three places: on the Home tab below your study card, in a small unit above the word card during a review, and on the page after a session. It shows no ads during onboarding or on your first day, and none after you buy Remove ads. We limit ads to content suitable for everyone (AdMob's "G" rating), matching the App's own rating.

**What Google's ads software collects.** To choose, show and measure ads and to prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK in the App automatically collects and sends to Google:

  * your device's IP address, which may be used to estimate its general location;
  * device identifiers: the Android advertising ID, the app set ID and, if applicable, other identifiers related to signed-in accounts on the device;
  * how you interact with ads and the App (for example app launches, ad views and taps); and
  * diagnostic information about the performance of the App and the ads software.

Google uses this information as described in [Google's Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) and on [How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites). You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings (usually Settings › Google › Ads, or Settings › Privacy › Ads; the path depends on your device); after you delete it, ads can no longer be personalized with it.

**Your consent.** Where the law requires consent for ads (for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), the App shows Google's consent message, provided through Google's User Messaging Platform, before the first ad. It never appears during onboarding. Your choices are stored on your device in the standard format of the IAB Transparency & Consent Framework, and you can change them at any time in Profile › Privacy choices. If you decline, you may still see ads that are not personalized, as Google allows. Where your consent is required, the App also uses this choice for analytics: it sends usage events to Mixpanel only if your choice allows it (purposes 1 and 8), sends nothing before you have chosen, and keeps analytics off if you decline. For users whose device is set up in the United States, ads in the App use Google's restricted data processing.

**Remove ads.** Remove ads is a one-time Google Play in-app purchase. Google handles the payment; the App asks Google Play for the price and whether you own it, whenever you open the App, so it can turn the ads off. After you buy it, the App stops loading ads and the ads software no longer starts.

**Permissions.** The Android App, and the Google software built into it, declare Android permissions in these groups: notifications, and restoring study reminders after your phone restarts; internet access and network status, which friend codes and the ads, analytics and purchase software need; Google Play purchases (billing); keeping the phone awake while background work runs; and, for the ads and analytics software, advertising and measurement permissions: the advertising ID, Android's ad services, the Google Play install referrer, and basic phone state, which lets the ads software read basic, non-sensitive phone information such as the type of mobile network, never your phone number, calls or contacts. The App does not ask for permission to read your contacts, location, microphone, camera, photos, files, calls or messages.

## 3. WHEN AND WITH WHOM DO WE SHARE INFORMATION?

We share information only with the companies that provide the services the App and the Site need:

  * **Google LLC** — in the Android App, provides Google AdMob, the Google Mobile Ads SDK and the User Messaging Platform described in Section 2, and Google Play's purchases. Google Cloud (Firebase) runs our friend-code server and its database, described under Friend codes in Section 1, and processes those records on our behalf; in the Android App, Google Play Integrity checks the device-check token. On Android, your phone's backup to Google, which includes the App's data as described in Section 1, is kept under your Google account; we have no access to it. See [Google's Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).
  * **Mixpanel, Inc.** — provides the product-analytics service that receives the usage events described under Usage events in Section 1, and processes them on our behalf so that we can read aggregated reports. See [Mixpanel's Privacy Policy](https://mixpanel.com/legal/privacy-policy/).
  * **Apple Inc.** — processes App Store purchases, provides the on-device frameworks the iPhone App uses (speech, notifications, widgets), checks the DeviceCheck token and keeps the device's bit for friend codes, described in Section 1, and keeps the copy of your progress in your iCloud, described in Section 1, under your Apple account; we have no access to it. See [Apple's Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).
  * **RevenueCat, Inc.** — records the purchases made in the App, as described under Purchase records (RevenueCat) in Section 1: the random install ID, the device information listed there, the IP address and the purchase records. It processes them on our behalf, so that we can see sales, refunds and conversion reports. See [RevenueCat's Privacy Policy](https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy).
  * **Cloudflare, Inc.** — hosts and delivers the Site and provides Cloudflare Web Analytics, described under Our website in Section 1. See [Cloudflare's Privacy Policy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/).

Who receives what: Mixpanel receives the usage events described in Section 1, and, in the Android App, Google receives the ads information described in Section 2. Apple, or Google on Android, processes the purchase you make, and RevenueCat receives its record, as described above. Google Cloud stores the friend-code records for us, and Apple or Google checks the device-check token. Cloudflare processes visits to the Site. We use neither your study data nor your usage events for advertising or cross-app tracking. We may disclose information if required by law, but as described above, we hold effectively no information about you to disclose, beyond what you email us, the order records Google Play gives us on Android, the purchase records RevenueCat keeps for us under the random install ID, and the friend-code records our server keeps under the anonymous install ID.

## 4. HOW LONG DO WE KEEP INFORMATION?

  * **Usage events:** kept by Mixpanel under its data retention settings, after which they are deleted. Aggregated reports may persist beyond that.
  * **Purchase records:** kept in RevenueCat under the random install ID until we delete them, for example at your request (Section 6). Deleting the App does not delete them; the ID is random and not linked to your identity.
  * **Friend-code records:** kept on our server under the anonymous install ID until we delete them, for example at your request (Section 6). Deleting the App does not delete them. The daily totals are not linked to any install. The IP address used to limit requests stays only briefly in the server's memory. Apple keeps the device's DeviceCheck bit under its own terms, and Google Cloud keeps any technical records of requests under its own policies.
  * **Ads information (Android):** kept by Google under its own retention policies; we receive only aggregated ad reports.
  * **On your device:** your study data, and on Android your ad consent choices, are kept until you delete the App.
  * **In your iCloud or Google backup:** the copy of your progress in your iCloud is kept until you delete it (Section 6), also after you delete the App; the App replaces it with each save. The copy in your phone's backup to Google is kept under your Google backup settings.
  * **Website visits:** Cloudflare Web Analytics reports cover the previous six months; Cloudflare keeps its records of requests to the Site under its own policies. We see only aggregated reports.
  * **Support email:** kept as long as needed to resolve your inquiry.

## 5. HOW DO WE KEEP INFORMATION SAFE?

Your study data stays on your device, protected by its operating system. Its copy in your iCloud is held and protected by Apple in your Apple account's private storage, which we cannot read; its copy in your backup to Google is held by Google and, as Section 1 describes, end-to-end encrypted with your screen lock (always on Android 12 and later; on Android 9 to 11, when your phone has a screen lock). Usage events and ads information are sent to Google, purchase records to RevenueCat, and friend-code information to our server, over encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS). Only our server can read or write the friend-code database, and it stores a one-way hash of your install token, not the token. We have implemented appropriate technical measures designed to protect the limited information we process. However, no electronic transmission over the internet can be guaranteed to be 100% secure.

## 6. WHAT ARE YOUR PRIVACY RIGHTS?

Depending on where you live (for example, the Republic of Korea, the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or California), you may have rights such as access, correction, deletion, and portability of your personal information, and the right to withdraw consent.

Because Lightning TOPIK keeps your study data on your device, with no study data on our side, you can delete it at any time by deleting the App, which removes it from your phone. To delete the copy in your iCloud, open iPhone Settings › your name › iCloud, open your iCloud storage, choose Lightning TOPIK, and delete its data; to stop the App from saving there, switch iCloud off for Lightning TOPIK in the same iCloud settings. The copy in your backup to Google follows your Google backup settings, where you can turn backup off or delete it. On Android, where the App asked for your consent, you can withdraw or change it in Profile › Privacy choices, and you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings. To have RevenueCat's records for your install deleted, email us, including the order number from your Apple or Google Play receipt if you made a purchase, and we will delete them in RevenueCat; deleting the App alone does not do this. To have our friend-code records for your install deleted, email us your friend code, which the App shows in Profile, and we will delete the records kept under that install's anonymous ID; deleting the App alone does not do this either. If you have any privacy question or request, contact us at [ung@sai-korean.com](mailto:ung@sai-korean.com) and we will respond in accordance with applicable data protection laws.

## 7. DO WE COLLECT INFORMATION FROM MINORS?

Our Services are not directed to children under 14 (or the equivalent minimum age in your jurisdiction), and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. The App has no accounts, no chat, no public profiles and no contact lists. Friend codes are anonymous codes that people may choose to share outside the App, and the App never shows who claimed a code. The iPhone App shows no advertising; the Android App, which shows ads, is listed on Google Play for users aged 18 and over. If you believe a child has provided us personal information through the support email, contact us and we will delete it.

## 8. INTERNATIONAL DATA TRANSFERS

Mixpanel, and in the Android App Google's ads services, may process information on servers located in the United States or other countries. RevenueCat is based in the United States and stores purchase records on Amazon Web Services servers there; its service providers may process them in other countries. Our friend-code server and its database run on Google Cloud in Singapore (region asia-southeast1); Apple's DeviceCheck and Google Play Integrity, which check the device-check tokens, may process them in the United States or other countries. Cloudflare delivers the Site from data centers around the world and may process visit information in the United States or other countries. Apple and Google keep the copy of your progress in your iCloud or Google backup where they store your account's data, under their own terms.
